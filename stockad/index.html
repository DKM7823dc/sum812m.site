Shopify didn't explode overnight. It compounded quietly first.

Shopify listed on the TSX and the NYSE on 21 May 2015 and raised roughly US$131 million. The consensus was that it was expensive, unproven, and about to be crushed by Amazon. It was none of those things.

This is the stretch that separates people who own ten-baggers from people who own screenshots. The business kept compounding while the share price fell hard, twice. Patience, not timing, paid for it.

THE ACCOUNT QUESTION

The wrapper matters as much as the ticker.

Most letters published south of the border never mention this. In Canada, where you hold a stock changes what you keep — and one rule in particular catches people out.

TFSAGrowth and Canadian dividends compound tax-free, and withdrawals don't affect your OAS or GIS. But a TFSA is not recognised as a retirement account by the IRS, so 15% is withheld on US dividends and you cannot reclaim it.
RRSPContributions are deductible now and growth is taxed only when you withdraw. With a valid W-8BEN on file, the US withholding on dividends is generally waived inside an RRSP — which is why the same US stock can be better held here.
CASHIn a non-registered account, eligible Canadian dividends receive the dividend tax credit. It's often the most efficient place for domestic payers — and the least efficient place for interest.
